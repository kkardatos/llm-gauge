## Key Quantum Principles

**Superposition**: Unlike classical bits that are either 0 or 1, quantum bits (qubits) can exist in multiple states simultaneously. A qubit can be 0, 1, or both at the same time until measured.

**Entanglement**: Qubits can become "entangled" - when two qubits are entangled, measuring one instantly determines the state of the other, regardless of distance. This creates powerful correlations between qubits.

**Interference**: Quantum states can interfere with each other, allowing some computational paths to cancel out while others amplify, directing the system toward correct answers.

## How It Works

Instead of processing information sequentially like traditional computers, quantum computers manipulate probability amplitudes of qubit states. They perform computations by:
1. Preparing qubits in superposition
2. Applying quantum gates (operations) that entangle and transform the qubits

Quantum computers excel at:
- **Optimization problems** (finding the best solution among many possibilities)

---

### 1. The Basic Unit: Bit vs. Qubit
To understand the difference, look at the smallest unit of information in each system.

*   **Classical Computer (Bit):** A bit can be either a **0** or a **1**. Think of a light switch—it's either ON or OFF. Everything on your computer is ultimately made up of 0s and 1s.
*   **Quantum Computer (Qubit):** A qubit can be a **0**, a **1**, or **both at the same time**.

**A. Superposition (The Spinning Coin)**
Imagine a coin spinning on a table. While it is spinning, is it Heads or Tails? It’s actually a blur of both. Only when you slap the coin down does it choose one state. A qubit in superposition is like that spinning coin; it can process many possibilities simultaneously before it "measures" to give a single answer.

**B. Entanglement (The Telepathy Link)**
When two qubits become entangled, they are linked so that the state of one instantly correlates with the state of the other, no matter how far apart they are. If you flip one coin and it becomes "Heads," its paired entangled qubit instantly becomes whatever is required by the correlation. This allows a quantum computer to process complex correlations between variables that classical computers struggle to track.

**C. Interference (Canceling Wrong Answers)**
Because qubits exist in waves of probability, they can be used like sound waves. If two wrong paths interfere destructively, they cancel each other out. If the right path interferes constructively, it gets amplified. The quantum computer uses these calculations to filter out incorrect solutions and highlight the correct one before the final "measurement."

A classical computer solves problems sequentially (one step after another) or in parallel using multiple cores (many sequential steps happening at once). A quantum computer does not simply run faster; it scales exponentially.

*   **2 qubits** can represent **4** states ($2^2$).
*   **30 qubits** can represent over **1 billion** different states simultaneously.
*   **50+ qubits** allows for processing power that dwarfs all current supercomputers combined for specific tasks.

*   **Artificial Intelligence:** Quantum machines could analyze massive datasets much faster to help train AI models more quickly.

```python
            arr[j + 1] = arr[j]
            j -= 1
        arr[j + 1] = key
    return arr

# Example:
print(insertion_sort([5, 3, 8, 4, 2]))
```
